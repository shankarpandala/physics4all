# Research Report 1 — How the World's Best Physics-Education Systems Teach Physics, Ages 6–18

A research synthesis based on national curriculum documents, TIMSS/PISA encyclopedias, and physics-education research. Systems selected for strongest outcomes: PISA 2022 science leaders (Singapore 561, Japan, Korea, Chinese Taipei, Hong Kong, Estonia, Finland historically) and IPhO powerhouses (China, Russia, Korea, Taiwan, India — see [PISA 2022 results](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-i_53f23881-en/full-report/how-did-countries-perform-in-pisa_dc514907.html) and the [IPhO country standings](https://ipho-unofficial.org/countries/)).

## 1. Per-country findings

### 1.1 Singapore

**Structure:** Integrated science P3–P6 (ages 9–12) → integrated lower-secondary science Sec 1–2 (ages 13–14) → discipline Physics from Sec 3 (O-Level Physics 6091, ages 15–16) → JC H1/H2 Physics 9749 (ages 17–18). The syllabus explicitly uses a **spiral approach**: "the revisiting of concepts and skills at different levels and with increasing depth" ([MOE Primary Science Syllabus 2023](https://www.moe.gov.sg/api/media/ba3562d3-5b31-4459-8693-45cde7b97273/Primary-Science-Syllabus-2023.pdf)).

**Primary (P3–P6), five themes — Diversity, Cycles, Systems, Energy, Interactions:**
- **P3:** diversity of materials (properties); interaction of forces — **magnets**; light and shadows in the lower block
- **P4:** matter (three states); **energy forms and uses: light** (sources, shadows, transparency) **and heat** (temperature vs heat, heat flow, expansion/contraction)
- **P5:** **electrical systems** (circuits, conductors/insulators, effects of components); water cycle
- **P6:** **energy conversion** (kinetic/potential/light/heat/electrical forms and transformations); **forces** (gravity, friction, elastic spring force, magnetic force)

**Lower secondary (Sec 1–2, 2021 syllabus)** — themes: Scientific Endeavour, Diversity, Models, Interactions, Systems. Physics content: Ray Model of Light; Model of Matter — particulate nature; Application of Forces and Transfer of Energy; Transfer of Heat Energy and its Effects; electrical systems ([MOE Lower Secondary Science Syllabus](https://www.moe.gov.sg/-/media/files/secondary/syllabuses/science/2021-science-lower-secondary-expressnormalacademic-syllabus.pdf)).

**O-Level Physics 6091 (ages 15–16)** — 20 topics in 6 sections ([SEAB 6091 syllabus](https://www.seab.gov.sg/files/O%20Lvl%20Syllabus%20Sch%20Cddts/2026/6091_y26_sy.pdf)):
1. *Measurement:* physical quantities, units, scalars/vectors
2. *Newtonian mechanics:* kinematics (graphs), dynamics (Newton's laws, friction), mass/weight/density, turning effect (moments, stability), pressure, energy/work/power
3. *Thermal:* kinetic particle model, thermal processes (conduction/convection/radiation), thermal properties (heat capacity, latent heat)
4. *Waves:* general wave properties, EM spectrum, light (reflection, refraction, lenses)
5. *Electricity & magnetism:* static electricity, current, DC circuits, practical electricity, magnetism, electromagnetism, EM induction
6. *Radioactivity:* nuclear structure, decay, half-life, safety

**A-Level H2 Physics 9749 (ages 17–18)** — the same backbone revisited mathematically: measurement; kinematics; dynamics; forces; work-energy-power; circular motion; gravitational field; temperature & ideal gases; first law of thermodynamics; oscillations; wave motion; superposition; electric fields; current; DC circuits; electromagnetism; EM induction; alternating current; quantum physics; nuclear physics ([SEAB 9749](https://www.seab.gov.sg/files/A%20Level%20Syllabus%20Sch%20Cddts/2025/9749_y25_sy.pdf)).

**Math sequencing:** O-Level physics is algebra + graph based (no calculus); vectors appear qualitatively at O-Level and quantitatively in H2. H2 Physics requires only elementary rates-of-change treatment; students take H2 Math (calculus) concurrently. **Distinctives:** lean, tightly specified syllabus; spiral revisiting; mastery orientation; structured problem sets and practical assessment.

### 1.2 China

**Structure:** Primary science grades 1–6 (integrated, from grade 1 since the 2017/2022 reform); **dedicated physics grades 8–9** under the [Compulsory Education Physics Curriculum Standards (2022 edition)](http://en.moe.gov.cn/news/press_releases/202205/t20220507_625532.html); senior high physics grades 10–12 under the [2017 (rev. 2020) standards](https://bksy.ecnu.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/51/eb/3c1adf774e569c6143b4e852b26a/f56bcf1e-17b8-45e8-9d07-420a9c7db99a.pdf), as 3 compulsory + 3 selective-compulsory modules.

**Junior high (PEP textbooks, chapter-by-chapter)** ([PEP e-textbook index](http://www.dzkbw.com/books/rjb/chuzhong-wuli/)):
- **Grade 8, sem 1:** 1. Mechanical motion (length/time, speed) 2. Sound 3. Changes of state 4. Light phenomena (propagation, reflection, plane mirrors, refraction, dispersion) 5. Lenses and applications 6. Mass and density
- **Grade 8, sem 2:** 7. Force 8. Motion and force (Newton's first law, inertia, friction) 9. Pressure (solids, liquids, atmospheric) 10. Buoyancy (Archimedes) 11. Work and mechanical energy 12. Simple machines
- **Grade 9:** 13. Internal energy 14. Uses of internal energy (heat engines) 15. Current and circuits 16. Voltage and resistance 17. Ohm's law 18. Electric power 19. Household electricity 20. Electricity and magnetism (motors, induction, generators) 21. Information transfer (EM waves) 22. Energy and sustainability

**Senior high (PEP 2019 textbooks):**
- **必修1:** describing motion; uniformly accelerated motion; interactions (gravity, elasticity, friction, force composition/decomposition); Newton's laws
- **必修2:** projectile/curvilinear motion; circular motion; universal gravitation and spaceflight; mechanical energy
- **必修3:** electrostatic field; potential and capacitance; circuits; electrical energy and closed-circuit Ohm's law; EM induction and EM waves (intro)
- **选择性必修1:** momentum conservation; mechanical oscillations (SHM); mechanical waves; optics (refraction, interference, diffraction)
- **选择性必修2:** magnetic fields (Ampère force, Lorentz force); EM induction; AC; sensors
- **选择性必修3:** kinetic theory; gases/solids/liquids; thermodynamics; atomic structure and wave-particle duality; the nucleus

**Assessment/culture:** In the Gaokao "3+1+2" model physics is the gateway subject for STEM; the **Chinese Physics Olympiad** three-stage pipeline feeds IPhO dominance. **Math sequencing:** junior physics uses one-formula arithmetic/algebra; vectors are introduced *inside physics* (grade 10); Gaokao physics avoids calculus but demands very deep multi-step algebraic problem solving. **Distinctives:** late but extremely fast complete two-year junior course; problem depth as the defining feature; full second theoretical pass in senior high.

### 1.3 Russia

**Structure:** The codified "early rigor" tradition: nature-study propaedeutics grades 1–4 → **dedicated physics from grade 7 (age 13) for all students** (Peryshkin line) → grades 10–11 second theoretical pass (Myakishev line), basic and profile (≥5 h/wk) tracks ([TIMSS Advanced Russia curriculum description](https://timssandpirls.bc.edu/timss2015/international-results/advanced/timss-advanced-2015/physics/curriculum/russian-federation-description-of-physics-programs-and-curriculum/)).

**Grades 7–9 (Peryshkin):**
- **Grade 7:** what physics studies, measurement; structure of matter (molecules, diffusion, states); interaction of bodies (motion, speed, inertia, mass, **density**, force, gravity, elastic force, friction); **pressure** of solids, liquids and gases (Pascal, atmospheric, Archimedes, floating); work, power, energy (levers, moments, "golden rule," efficiency, KE/PE)
- **Grade 8:** thermal phenomena (internal energy, heat transfer, specific heat, phase changes, heat engines); electrical phenomena (charge, current, voltage, resistance, Ohm's law, work/power of current, Joule heating); electromagnetic phenomena (magnetic field of current, electromagnets, motors); light phenomena (propagation, reflection, mirrors, refraction, lenses, the eye)
- **Grade 9:** laws of motion (kinematics with vectors, uniformly accelerated motion, Newton's laws, free fall, momentum, jet propulsion, energy conservation); oscillations, waves, sound; electromagnetic field (induction, EM waves, light as EM wave, dispersion, spectra); atom and nucleus (radioactivity, nuclear reactions, nuclear energy); elements of astrophysics

Grades 7–9 constitute a **complete first pass of all of physics** with quantitative problem solving from day one.

**Grades 10–11 (Myakishev):**
- **Grade 10:** mechanics rigorously (kinematics, dynamics, conservation laws, statics); molecular physics and thermal phenomena (kinetic theory, ideal gas, thermodynamics); electrodynamics I (electrostatics, DC current, current in various media)
- **Grade 11:** magnetic field and induction; EM oscillations and waves, AC; optics (geometric + wave, elements of relativity); quantum physics (photoelectric effect, atom, nucleus, particles); astronomy/structure of the universe

**Math sequencing:** algebra begins in grade 7 exactly when physics begins; vectors (grades 8–9 geometry) arrive just before vector kinematics in physics 9; trig and derivatives (grades 10–11) support oscillations and mechanics. Russian school physics is "highly mathematics-intensive," with physics class the designated arena for *applying* mathematics ([ZDM study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11858-021-01248-z)).

**Distinctives:** earliest universal dedicated physics; the **задачник (problem-book) culture** (Peryshkin, Rymkevich); the **physics-mathematics school** tradition — [Kolmogorov's AESC at MSU](https://internat.msu.ru/en/), [Lyceum 239](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Physics_and_Mathematics_Lyceum_No._239), [MIPT Phystech schools](https://mipt.ru/english/edu/phystechschools/) — with olympiad pipelines.

### 1.4 Japan

**Structure:** Science from grade 3; elementary science split into **"Matter and Energy"** and "Life and the Earth" strands; lower secondary "first field" (physics/chemistry); upper secondary 物理基礎 (Basic Physics) then 物理 (Advanced Physics) ([TIMSS 2023 Japan](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Japan.pdf), [MEXT elementary course of study (EN)](https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20220405-mxt_kyoiku02-000005241_003.pdf)).

**Elementary "Energy" strand:** G3 — object and weight; forces of wind and rubber; light and sound; magnets; electric circuits. G4 — compression of air and water; thermal expansion; current and dry cells. G5 — **motion of pendulums** (controlled-variable experiments); electromagnets. G6 — levers; electricity generation, storage and conversion. One phenomenon per year, with escalating experimental control (compare → relate variables → control variables).

**Lower secondary:** G7 — light (reflection, refraction, lenses), sound, force (springs, pressure). G8 — electric currents (circuits, V–I, resistance, power) and magnetic fields. G9 — motion and energy (force composition, regularities of motion, work, mechanical energy).

**Upper secondary:** 物理基礎: motion and forces, energy, thermal, waves, electricity — algebra-based. 物理: full mechanics (projectiles, momentum, circular motion, SHM), thermodynamics, waves incl. interference/diffraction, fields, EM induction/AC, atomic/nuclear ([EJU physics syllabus](https://www.jasso.go.jp/en/ryugaku/eju/examinee/syllabus/science_phy.html)). Calculus is formally in Math III but school physics uses pre-derived formulas plus trig/vectors.

### 1.5 South Korea

**Structure:** 6-3-3; integrated science grades 3–9 organized by "core concepts" across grade bands; grade 10 Integrated Science; electives Physics I / Physics II ([TIMSS 2023 Korea](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Korea.pdf)).

**Grades 3–9:** *Force and motion:* G3–4 gravity/weight, friction, balance; G5–6 speed, uniform motion; G7–9 free fall, elastic and buoyant force, PE/KE, conservation of mechanical energy. *Electricity and magnetism:* G3–4 magnets; G5–6 circuits, electromagnets, motors, generators; G7–9 electric force, electrification, circuits with V/I/R, magnetic field. *Heat and energy:* G5–6 temperature, conduction/convection; G7–9 thermal equilibrium, specific heat, expansion, power, energy conversion. *Waves:* G3–4 sound, light travels straight, shadows, mirrors; G5–6 reflection; G7–9 wave types, amplitude, frequency, refraction, lenses, color.

**High school:** *Physics I* — mechanics and energy (momentum, conservation, thermodynamics basics, **qualitative special relativity**), matter and electromagnetic field (band theory/semiconductors), waves and information (wave-particle duality). *Physics II* — full quantitative: torque, projectile/circular motion, Kepler, fields and potential, capacitors, induction, AC, wave optics, modern physics. Physics I is deliberately modern-physics-flavored to attract students; Physics II restores mathematical depth.

### 1.6 Finland

**Structure:** Integrated **environmental studies** grades 1–6; **physics as a separate subject grades 7–9**; modular upper secondary with 2 compulsory + 6 optional physics modules ([TIMSS 2023 Finland](https://timss2023.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Finland.pdf)).

- **Grades 7–9, six content areas:** scientific research (planning experiments, measuring); physics in daily life; physics in society (energy production); physics shaping the worldview; **interaction and motion** (forces, models of constant and changing motion, work and power); **electricity** (V–I relationship, circuits, charge, magnetism, generation).
- **Upper secondary modules:** FY1 Physics as a natural science (quantities, SI, graphical models); FY2 Physics, environment and society (energy); FY3 Energy and heat; FY4 Force and motion; FY5 Periodic motion and waves; FY6 Electricity; FY7 Electromagnetism and light; FY8 Matter, radiation and quantisation ([Scientix EN translation](http://storage.eun.org/resources/upload/482/20191112_140830068_482_Finnish_curriculum_Scientix_English-Nov2019.pdf)).
- **Distinctives:** **phenomenon-based learning** (mandatory multidisciplinary real-world module yearly); physics taught from observed phenomena toward models; late formal physics, minimal early formalism — with top PISA results.

### 1.7 Estonia

**Structure:** Integrated science grades 1–7; **physics grades 8–9**; gymnasium (10–12) with 5 compulsory physics courses ([Basic school curriculum, Annex 4 (EN)](https://www.hm.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2022-10/est_basic_school_nat_cur_2014_appendix_4_final.pdf); [Upper secondary, Annex 4](https://www.hm.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2022-10/est_upper_secondary_nat_cur_2014_appendix_4_final.pdf)).

**Basic school physics (8–9), four blocks (optics first is distinctive):** 1. Study of Light (grade 8): sources, spectra, propagation, reflection, refraction, lenses, the eye. 2. Mechanics (8–9): motion, speed, inertia, density, force, balance, pressure, buoyancy, work/energy; l = vt, ρ = m/V. 3. Electricity (9): charge, field, current, circuits, Ohm's law, work/power, magnetism. 4. Thermodynamics + nuclear energy (9): particle model, temperature, heat transfer, heat capacity, internal energy; nuclear basics. Explicitly inquiry-based: research questions, experiments, data processing with uncertainty, tasks combining up to two formulas.

**Gymnasium, 5 courses:** 1. Introduction + kinematics; 2. Mechanics (dynamics, conservation, oscillations/waves); 3. Electromagnetism; 4. Energy (thermal + electrical energy production); 5. Micro- and mega-world physics (quantum, nuclear, cosmology).

### 1.8 United Kingdom (England)

**Structure:** Science compulsory from age 5: KS1 → KS2 → KS3 → GCSE → A-level ([national curriculum programmes of study](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study)).

**Primary physics topics:** Y1–2 everyday materials. Y3 light and shadows; forces and magnets. Y4 sound; electricity (simple circuits); states of matter. Y5 Earth and space; forces (gravity, resistance, mechanisms). Y6 light (straight-line propagation); electricity (circuit symbols, voltage effects).

**KS3 (Y7–9):** energy (transfers and stores, power); motion and forces (speed, graphs, resultants, moments, Hooke's law, pressure); waves (sound and light, ray model); electricity and electromagnetism (current, PD, resistance, static, magnets, motors); matter (particle model); space physics ([KS3 PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7d563de5274a2af0ae2ffa/SECONDARY_national_curriculum_-_Science_220714.pdf)).

**GCSE (Y10–11):** energy; electricity; particle model; atomic structure and radioactivity; forces (quantitative Newtonian mechanics); waves incl. EM spectrum; magnetism and electromagnetism; space physics.

**A-level (AQA 7408):** Year 1 — measurements, **particles and radiation** (early modern-physics unit: quarks, leptons, photons), waves, mechanics and materials, electricity. Year 2 — further mechanics (circular motion, SHM), thermal, **fields** (gravitational, electric, magnetic, capacitance, induction), nuclear, plus one option ([AQA spec](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/a-level/physics-7408/specification/specification-at-a-glance)). Calculus not formally required; graphical rates-of-change reasoning is.

### 1.9 Germany (Bavarian Gymnasium)

**Structure:** Primary Sachunterricht; "Natur und Technik" grades 5–7 (grade 7: optics, circuits, physical methods); **Physik grades 8–13** ([LehrplanPLUS](https://www.lehrplanplus.bayern.de/fachlehrplan/gymnasium/8/physik)).

- **Grade 8:** electric current (I, V, R, series/parallel); optics (reflection, refraction, lens imaging); mechanics (velocity, acceleration, Newton's 2nd law, force composition, Hooke's law)
- **Grade 9:** **energy as a conserved quantity** (mechanical & electrical energy, work, power, efficiency); **atoms** (photon model, spectra, energy levels — unusually early quantum); **heat** (absolute temperature, internal energy, heat transfer, greenhouse effect)
- **Grade 10:** electromagnetism (fields, motor, induction, generator, transformer); **momentum conservation**; kinematics revisited deeply (graphs, free fall, projectiles); **nuclear physics** (decay, half-life, E = mc²)
- **Grade 11:** circular motion and gravitation; oscillations and waves (SHM, diffraction, interference, double slit); special relativity insights; astronomy
- **Grades 12–13:** fields, induction/AC, quantum physics, atomic and nuclear physics, optional astrophysics — with derivatives from the parallel Analysis course

**Distinctives:** energy as the explicit organizing spine; early photon model; continuous mathematical escalation.

### 1.10 India

**Structure:** Integrated science 6–10 (NCERT), discipline physics 11–12; the JEE/NEET ecosystem overlays 11–12.

**Physics in integrated science:** C6 motion and measurement; light, shadows; electricity and circuits; magnets. C7 heat; motion and time (speed, graphs); electric current effects; light. C8 force and pressure; friction; sound; chemical effects of current; lightning/charges; light. C9 motion (graphs, equations); force and laws of motion; gravitation (universal law, buoyancy); work and energy; sound. C10 light — reflection and refraction (mirror/lens formulas); the human eye; electricity (Ohm's law, circuits, power); magnetic effects of current.

**Classes 11–12 (calculus-based for all science-stream students):**
- **Class 11:** physical world and measurement; kinematics (straight line, plane/vectors); laws of motion; work, energy and power; system of particles and rotational motion; gravitation; properties of bulk matter (elasticity, fluids, thermal); thermodynamics; kinetic theory; oscillations and waves
- **Class 12:** electrostatics; current electricity; magnetic effects and magnetism; EM induction; AC; EM waves; ray optics; wave optics; dual nature of radiation and matter; atoms; nuclei; semiconductor electronics

**Distinctives:** NCERT physics 11 introduces differentiation/integration *before* the math course completes them; the JEE Advanced problem culture (multi-concept, Irodov-style) functions as a mass olympiad pipeline (India tied for first in medals at IPhO 2026 with 5 golds).

### 1.11 International benchmarks: IB and AP

**IB:** MYP prescribes a concept-driven framework without a fixed content list. **DP Physics (2023, first exams 2025)** — 5 themes: A. Space, time and motion (kinematics, forces, momentum, work/energy + HL rigid bodies and relativity); B. Particulate nature of matter (thermal, gas laws, circuits + HL thermodynamics); C. Wave behaviour (SHM, waves, standing waves, Doppler); D. Fields (gravitational, electric, magnetic + HL induction); E. Nuclear and quantum (atomic structure + HL quantum, decay, fission/fusion and stars). SL 150 h / HL 240 h; no calculus required.

**AP (2024–25 revision):** *Physics 1* (algebra-based): kinematics; dynamics; circular motion and gravitation; energy; momentum; SHM; torque and rotation; **fluids**. *Physics 2*: thermodynamics; electric force/field/potential; circuits; magnetism and induction; geometric and physical optics; modern physics. *Physics C* (Mechanics, E&M): calculus-based university-level, taken with AP Calculus ([College Board revisions](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-physics/revisions-2024-25)).

## 2. Synthesis: the consensus staging, age 6–18

Across all eleven systems the same deep structure appears — a **three-pass spiral** over a universal backbone: **mechanics → matter/heat → sound/waves → light → electricity & magnetism → modern physics**, revisited at (1) phenomenon level, (2) one-formula quantitative level, (3) theory/multi-step level.

| Age | Stage | Consensus physics content |
|---|---|---|
| 6–8 | Early primary | Materials and properties; pushes/pulls; magnets; light/shadow; sound as vibration; simple circuits |
| 9–12 | Upper primary | Forces incl. friction/gravity qualitatively; levers/balance; heat and temperature; states of matter; light reflection; circuits; energy forms and conversions; pendulum/electromagnet investigations; Earth-space basics |
| 12–15 | Lower secondary (first full pass) | Measurement, density → motion and speed (graphs) → forces, pressure, buoyancy → work/energy/machines → particle model, heat transfer, phase change → sound/waves → geometric optics → circuits, Ohm's law, power → electromagnetism → intro atomic/nuclear + energy resources |
| 15–16 | Transition (O-Level/GCSE/G10) | Same strands quantitatively: Newton's laws, moments, kinetic theory, EM spectrum, EM induction, radioactivity |
| 16–18 | Upper secondary (theory pass) | Vector kinematics/dynamics → circular motion, gravitation → momentum & energy conservation → (rotation/statics advanced) → kinetic theory & thermodynamics → SHM, waves, superposition, wave optics → electrostatics, DC circuits, magnetic fields, induction, AC → quantum, atomic, nuclear (→ relativity, astro as extensions) |

Two structural variants both work: the **long ramp** (UK/Japan/Korea: physics spread thinly across integrated science) and the **short intensive course** (Russia G7–9, China G8–9 as a dedicated subject with systematic problem sets). The intensive-dedicated model correlates with the strongest olympiad pipelines.

**Math–physics co-sequencing consensus:** ages 6–12 no symbolic math (measurement and graphing only); 12–15 proportional reasoning + single formulas synchronized with first-year algebra (Russia's grade-7 simultaneity is the archetype); 15–16 trig and vector components arrive with projectile/force-decomposition problems; 16–18 systems split calculus-based (Russia profile, India, AP C, German Oberstufe) vs algebra/trig-based (Singapore H2, IB, A-level, AP 1/2, Japan) — both produce elite results.

## 3. Key pedagogical principles extracted

1. **Spiral curriculum is the universal architecture** — validated empirically by learning-progressions research: energy understanding develops through ordered levels, and mastery of early energy units predicted 42–68% of variance in later learning ([Herrmann-Abell & DeBoer](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/Herrmann-Abell_DeBoer_JRST_062017_e-offprint.pdf); [Kaldaras et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/tea.21672)).
2. **Phenomena before formalism.** Equations are attached to phenomena students have already manipulated — never the reverse. Systems differ only in how long the phenomenon phase lasts.
3. **A complete qualitative-quantitative tour of ALL of physics by ~15.** The Russian and Chinese models prove it works — provided problems stay one-formula deep.
4. **Energy as the unifying spine**, escalated continuously from age 8 to 18.
5. **Problem-solving culture is the differentiator at the top** (Russian задачник tradition, CPhO pipeline, JEE ecosystem): a lean standard curriculum plus a voluntary high-ceiling problem track.
6. **Synchronize, don't wait for, mathematics.** Each math tool arrives at most one year before (or exactly when) physics needs it; no strong system introduces symbolic formulas before ~age 12.
7. **Experimental skill has its own progression:** compare → control variables → evaluate uncertainty → design investigations. Top systems assess it formally.
8. **Modern physics: end-loaded core, early-taste motivator** (Germany's grade-9 photon model, AQA's year-1 particle physics, Korea's Physics-I relativity).
9. **Late specialization does not harm literacy; early specialization builds elites.** The best-rounded designs run both: a spiral mass curriculum plus selective problem streams.
10. **Keep the mass syllabus lean, the assessment sharp.** Top outcomes come from a small, coherent core taught to mastery with strong labs and problem work — not topic maximalism.
