# Research Report 2 — How the World's Best Universities Structure the Undergraduate Physics Degree

*Course structures verified against live catalogs/handbooks, July 2026.*

## 1. Per-program course sequences

### 1.1 MIT — Physics (Course 8)
Sources: [MIT Catalog degree chart](https://catalog.mit.edu/degree-charts/physics-course-8/), [Course 8 subjects](https://catalog.mit.edu/subjects/8/), [MIT Physics dept](https://physics.mit.edu/academic-programs/undergrads/). Nearly every course has full materials on [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu).

| Year | Courses |
|---|---|
| 1 Fall | **8.01 Physics I** (Newtonian mechanics) + **18.01/18.02** (single/multivariable calculus) |
| 1 Spring | **8.02 Physics II** (electromagnetism, fields, circuits) + **18.03 Differential Equations** |
| 2 Fall | **8.03 Physics III** (vibrations, waves, optics) + **8.033 Relativity** + **8.223 Classical Mechanics II** (Lagrangian & Hamiltonian) |
| 2 Spring | **8.04 Quantum Physics I** (wave mechanics; prereq 8.03 + 18.03) + **8.044 Statistical Physics I** |
| 3 Fall | **8.05 Quantum Physics II** (Dirac formalism, spin) + **8.13 Junior Lab** |
| 3 Spring | **8.06 Quantum Physics III** (perturbation theory, scattering) + **8.14** |
| 3–4 | Electives: **8.07 Electromagnetism II**, **8.08 Statistical Physics II**, **8.09 Classical Mechanics III**, **18.06 Linear Algebra**, 8.286 Early Universe, 8.323 QFT I (grad) |

**Key structural fact:** MIT runs *three passes* at mechanics (8.01 → 8.223 → 8.09), *three* at QM (8.04 → 8.05 → 8.06), and puts special relativity (8.033) unusually early — sophomore fall.

### 1.2 Cambridge — Natural Sciences Tripos (physics path) and Mathematical Tripos
Sources: [NST Part IA](https://www.natsci.tripos.cam.ac.uk/course-structure/year-one-part-ia), [IB](https://www.natsci.tripos.cam.ac.uk/course-structure/year-two-part-ib), [II](https://www.natsci.tripos.cam.ac.uk/course-structure/year-three-part-ii), [Maths Tripos Schedules 2025–26](https://www.maths.cam.ac.uk/undergrad/files/schedules.pdf).

- **Part IA:** Physics (mechanics, oscillations, waves, fields, intro SR and quantum, labs) + Mathematics (vector calculus, matrices, complex numbers, ODEs, probability).
- **Part IB:** Physics A (waves/optics, QM, condensed matter, experimental methods), Physics B (dynamics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics) + Mathematics (group theory, tensors, Fourier transforms, calculus of variations, complex variables) + compulsory Python.
- **Part II:** core = Advanced Quantum Physics, Thermal and Statistical Physics, Electrodynamics & Optics, Relativity (+ GR); options = Particle & Nuclear, Quantum Condensed Matter, Astrophysical Fluid Dynamics, Computational project. **Part III (MSci)** = research-level options.
- **Mathematical Tripos physics path:** IA — Vectors and Matrices; Differential Equations; Vector Calculus; Dynamics and Relativity; Probability. IB — Methods (Fourier, PDEs, Green functions); Quantum Mechanics; Electromagnetism; Fluid Dynamics; Variational Principles; Complex Methods; Linear Algebra. II — Classical Dynamics; Principles/Applications of Quantum Mechanics; Statistical Physics; Electrodynamics; General Relativity; Cosmology.

### 1.3 Oxford — BA/MPhys Physics
Sources: [course listing](https://www.ox.ac.uk/admissions/undergraduate/courses/course-listing/physics), [exam regulations](https://examregs.admin.ox.ac.uk/Regulation?code=pexaminphys).

- **Year 1:** CP1 Classical mechanics + special relativity; CP2 Electromagnetism, circuits, optics; CP3/CP4 Mathematical Methods (complex numbers, ODEs, vectors, matrices; multiple integrals, vector calculus, normal modes, wave equation); labs.
- **Year 2:** A1 Thermal physics (kinetic theory, thermodynamics); A2 Electromagnetism and optics; A3 Quantum physics; math methods; labs.
- **Year 3:** B1 Fluids; B2 Symmetry and Relativity; B3 Atomic and Laser Physics; B4 Sub-atomic Physics; **B5 General Relativity**; B6 Condensed-Matter Physics; computational/experimental projects.
- **Year 4 (MPhys):** two major options (astrophysics, quantum information, condensed matter, particle physics, theoretical physics, …) + project.

**Key structural fact:** Oxford front-loads a dedicated two-paper math-methods sequence in year 1 and reaches GR/particle physics as *standard third-year papers*.

### 1.4 Germany — LMU Munich BSc Physik (the E/T parallel-track model)
Source: [official Studienplan](https://cms-cdn.lmu.de/media/17-physik/17-fakultaet-physik/studienplan_bsc_physik_bis_ws22-23.pdf). Three parallel tracks — experimental (E), theoretical (T), mathematics (M):

| Sem | Experimental | Theoretical | Math |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | E1 Mechanik | R Rechenmethoden (just-in-time vector calc, ODEs) | M1 Analysis & Linear Algebra I |
| 2 | E2 Wärme u. Elektromagnetismus | **T1 Theoretische Mechanik** | M2 Analysis & LA II |
| 3 | E3 EM-Wellen und Optik | **T2 Quantenmechanik** | M3 Analysis III |
| 4 | E4 Atom- und Molekülphysik | **T3 Elektrodynamik** | M4 Numerik |
| 5 | E5 Kern- und Teilchenphysik + E6 Festkörperphysik | **T4 Statistische Physik** | — |
| 6 | Thesis + electives | | |

**Key structural fact:** every subject is taught **twice in parallel** — phenomenologically (E) then theoretically (T), the T-course lagging by 1–2 semesters. (Heidelberg's PTP1–PTP4 is the same model.)

### 1.5 India — IIT structure
Sources: [IIT Hyderabad BTech Engineering Physics curriculum](https://physics.iith.ac.in/assets/docs/Annex.8_BTech%20Eng%20Physics%20Curriculum%20&%20course%20approvals.pdf), [IIT Kanpur BS Physics](https://www.iitk.ac.in/phyold/index.php/about-us/8-department/64-bs-in-physics).

IIT Hyderabad spine: Sem 1 — Calculus, **Maths for Physics** (vector calculus through residues), Modern Physics. Sem 2 — Linear Algebra, Differential Equations, **Electricity & Magnetism** (Griffiths ch. 1–9). Sem 3 — **Classical Mechanics** (Lagrangian/Hamiltonian through Hamilton–Jacobi), **Special Relativity** (with tensors), Probability. Sem 4 — **Thermodynamics**, **Advanced Mathematical Physics** (group theory), **Modern Optics**, **Computational Physics**. Sem 5 — **Quantum Mechanics 1** (through perturbation theory), **Electrodynamics** (full Griffiths + Jackson intro), **Statistical Mechanics** (ensembles, quantum gases, Ising). Sem 6 — **Solid State**, **QM II** (scattering, symmetries), **Atomic & Molecular**. Sem 7 — **Nuclear**, **Particle Physics**. Sem 8 — electives (QFT, GR, Advanced Stat Mech, Astrophysics).

### 1.6 Russia — the Landau tradition (two parallel spines)
Sources: [Landau chair at MIPT — theoretical minimum](https://chair.itp.ac.ru/index.php?sub=minimum&lang=en), [Course of Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_of_Theoretical_Physics).

1. **General physics** (experiment-first; Sivukhin's 5 volumes; problems from Irodov): Mechanics → Thermodynamics & Molecular Physics → Electricity → Optics → Atomic & Nuclear (semesters 1–5).
2. **Theoretical physics** (Landau & Lifshitz), from ~semester 3: Theoretical Mechanics → Classical Field Theory (SR + E&M + GR) → Quantum Mechanics → Statistical Physics, continuing into the graduate volumes.

**Landau's Theoretical Minimum** = 11 exams on the L&L course: Mechanics (vol. 1) by sem 3; Field Theory (vol. 2) by sem 5; QM (vol. 3) by sem 6; Statistical Physics I (vol. 5) at master's; QED (4), Continuous Media (6–8), Stat Phys II (9), Kinetics (10) at PhD level.

## 2. The canonical self-study roadmaps

- **'t Hooft, ["How to become a GOOD theoretical physicist"](https://goodtheorist.science/):** Languages → Primary Mathematics → Classical Mechanics → Optics → Statistical Mechanics & Thermodynamics → Electronics → Electromagnetism → Computational Physics → Quantum Mechanics → Atoms & Molecules → Solid State → Nuclear → Plasma → Advanced Mathematics → Special Relativity → Advanced QM → Phenomenology → General Relativity → Cosmology → Astro → QFT → SUSY/Supergravity → Superstrings.
- **Susan Rigetti, ["So You Want to Learn Physics"](https://www.susanrigetti.com/physics):** (1) Intro Mechanics — Young & Freedman + calculus; (2) Electrostatics — Y&F; (3) Waves & Vibrations — French + King (math: Zill); (4) Modern Physics — Y&F; (5) Classical Mechanics — Taylor (+ Morin); (6) Electrodynamics — Griffiths (+ Schey); (7) Quantum Mechanics — Griffiths; (8) Thermal/Statistical — Schroeder; (9) Electives — Hecht, Griffiths particles, Carroll & Ostlie, Ryden, Zwiebach. Graduate: Arfken → Jackson → Sakurai → Pathria → Carroll GR → Zee/Peskin.
- **Susskind, [The Theoretical Minimum](https://theoreticalminimum.com/courses):** 1. Classical Mechanics, 2. Quantum Mechanics, 3. Special Relativity & Electrodynamics, 4. General Relativity, 5. Cosmology, 6. Statistical Mechanics + supplemental particle physics/strings courses.

## 3. The consensus undergraduate core

**(a) Intro/general physics with calculus (Year 1):** Newtonian mechanics; E&M with circuits; waves/oscillations; intro thermal; intro modern. = MIT 8.01/8.02, Oxford CP1/CP2, Cambridge IA, LMU E1/E2, Sivukhin 1–3, IIT year 1.

**(b) The theoretical core four (Years 2–3), in near-invariant order:**
1. **Classical Mechanics** (Lagrangian/Hamiltonian) — always first
2. **Electrodynamics** (full Maxwell, radiation, relativistic formulation)
3. **Quantum Mechanics** (2–3 semesters: wave mechanics → Dirac formalism → perturbation/scattering)
4. **Statistical Mechanics/Thermal** — always last or tied-last

**(c) Waves & optics:** its own explicit course everywhere — the load-bearing bridge from mechanics to both QM and E&M (MIT 8.03 is the prerequisite to both 8.04 and 8.044).

**(d) Special relativity:** taught early (Oxford CP1 year 1, MIT 8.033 sophomore fall), then again inside electrodynamics.

**(e) Math methods:** a dedicated physics-facing course in every program.

**(f) Labs & computation:** a continuous spine everywhere; computation now compulsory.

**(g) Advanced electives (Years 3–4):** solid state (universal), nuclear/particle, atomic/molecular, GR (standard UG material in the UK), astrophysics/cosmology, fluids, QFT-intro as the graduate gateway.

## 4. Dependency map: math → physics

| Math topic | Must precede | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Single-variable calculus | Intro mechanics (concurrent OK) | MIT 18.01 ∥ 8.01 |
| Multivariable calculus | Intro E&M | MIT 18.02 before/with 8.02; Oxford CP4 ∥ CP2 |
| **Vector calculus** (div/grad/curl, Gauss/Stokes) | **Electrodynamics** (hard prerequisite) | Oxford CP4; Cambridge IA Vector Calculus before IB Electromagnetism |
| **ODEs** | Waves, QM, stat mech | MIT 18.03 is the formal prerequisite for 8.04 and 8.044 |
| **Linear algebra** (eigenvalues, Hermitian operators) | **QM formalism** | MIT 18.06; LMU M1/M2 before T2 |
| **Calculus of variations** | **Lagrangian mechanics** | Cambridge Variational Principles feeds Classical Dynamics |
| Fourier series & transforms | Waves, QM, optics, PDEs | Cambridge IB Methods before Part II physics |
| **PDEs** + special functions (Legendre, Bessel, spherical harmonics) | Advanced E&M (Laplace), QM in 3D (hydrogen) | Cambridge Methods; Jackson-level E&M |
| **Complex analysis** (residues) | Advanced QM (scattering, Green functions) | Cambridge IB Complex Methods |
| **Probability & statistics** | **Statistical mechanics**, lab analysis | MIT 8.044 opens with probability |
| **Tensors & index notation** | SR (four-vectors) → GR | Oxford B2 → B5; Landau vol. 2 |
| **Group theory** (Lie groups, representations) | Advanced QM, particle physics, QFT | Cambridge IB Maths; IITH Advanced Math Physics before particles |
| Numerical methods | Computational physics, labs | LMU M4; IITH Computational Physics |

**Critical path:** calculus → (vector calculus → E&M), (ODEs + linear algebra → QM), (variational calculus → Lagrangian mechanics), (probability → stat mech), (PDEs/special functions → QM-in-3D and advanced E&M), (tensors → SR → GR), (complex analysis + group theory → the graduate gateway).

## 6. Design takeaways for a self-study curriculum

1. **Two-pass architecture is universal**: phenomenological first pass (general physics), then theoretical second pass (the core four) — explicit at LMU and in the Russian general/theoretical split, implicit in MIT's 8.01 → 8.223 → 8.09 laddering. Do not start with Landau.
2. **Order of the theoretical core is invariant**: Lagrangian mechanics → electrodynamics → quantum mechanics (2 passes minimum) → statistical mechanics.
3. **Math must lead by one step, not by years** — every program teaches math "just in time."
4. **SR early, GR late.**
5. **Waves is the hinge course.**
6. **Take the union of the UK reach (GR + particles as standard year-3) and the US depth (three semesters of QM)**: QM II + full electrodynamics + statistical mechanics as the spine, then solid state, particles, GR, and a QFT-lite gateway as electives — exactly the 't Hooft/Rigetti consensus.
