# Research Report 3 — The Complete Mathematics Pathway: From Counting to the Math of an Advanced Physics Degree

Sources: official Singapore MOE/SEAB syllabi, Common Core standards, NCERT/CBSE syllabi, the Russian (TIMSS-documented) curriculum, Khan Academy's actual course ladder, MIT's course-8 catalog prerequisites, and the verified tables of contents of Boas, Riley/Hobson/Bence, and Arfken/Weber/Harris. Full URL list at the end.

## Part 1: The school-math progression (what to re-master, stage by stage)

All four high-performing systems examined (Singapore, Russia, US Common Core, Indian NCERT) converge on the same dependency chain.

- **Stage 1 — Number sense and additive arithmetic (K–2 / Singapore P1–P2):** counting, place value, number bonds, addition/subtraction with regrouping, intro multiplication/division, measurement, shapes, graphs. Singapore's CPA (Concrete–Pictorial–Abstract) approach and **bar-model method** are worth an adult's time: bar modeling is proto-algebra and trains exactly the "set up the equation from the situation" skill physics demands.
- **Stage 2 — Multiplicative arithmetic (Grades 3–4):** times tables to automaticity, multi-digit multiplication/division, order of operations, factors/multiples, fraction equivalence, decimals, angles, area/perimeter, mental arithmetic and estimation (feeds physics order-of-magnitude checks).
- **Stage 3 — The rational-number wall (Grades 4–6):** all four operations on fractions, decimal operations, fraction↔decimal↔percent, percentage change, **ratio and rate, speed–distance–time**, averages, composite areas, volume. *The single most failure-prone band for adults and the one that most directly feeds physics.*
- **Stage 4 — Integers, proportional reasoning, pre-algebra (Grades 6–8):** negative numbers, exponents/roots/scientific notation, **proportional relationships as a formal object (y = kx)** — Common Core deliberately spends most of grades 6–7 here — variables, linear equations, coordinate plane, first functions, Pythagorean theorem, basic probability/statistics.
- **Stage 5 — Algebra 1 (Grades 8–9):** Khan Academy's 16 units incl. **working with units (dimensional analysis)**, linear equations/graphs, systems, functions, sequences, exponents & radicals, exponential growth/decay, quadratics. Mastery target: fluent, error-free symbolic manipulation — isolate any variable in any formula.
- **Stage 6 — Geometry (Grades 9–10):** congruence/similarity (the geometric face of proportional reasoning), right-triangle trig, circles, solids, coordinate geometry, transformations. An adult needs the *results* and spatial fluency, not proof-writing stamina.
- **Stage 7 — Algebra 2 (Grades 10–11):** polynomials, complex numbers, rational exponents, **exponentials & logarithms as inverse functions (log rules to automaticity — entropy, decay, dB, Boltzmann factors)**, transformations of functions, modeling.
- **Stage 8 — Trigonometry:** unit circle from memory; radians; sin/cos/tan graphs with amplitude/period/phase (*this IS the mathematics of waves*); Pythagorean/sum/double-angle identities; law of sines/cosines; inverse trig. Singapore's O-Level Additional Mathematics ([SEAB 4049](https://www.seab.gov.sg/files/O%20Lvl%20Syllabus%20Sch%20Cddts/2025/4049_y25_sy.pdf)) confirms strong systems give students basic calculus with kinematics *before* university.
- **Stage 9 — Precalculus (Grades 11–12):** composite/inverse functions, complex numbers in polar form, conic basics, **vectors**, matrices, probability/combinatorics, **series and the binomial theorem**, limits. NCERT Class 11–12 and Russia's grades 10–11 "Algebra and Beginnings of Analysis" ([TIMSS Advanced documentation](https://timssandpirls.bc.edu/timss2015/international-results/advanced/timss-advanced-2015/mathematics/programs-and-curriculum/russian-federation-description-of-advanced-mathematics-programs-and-curricula/)) show strong national systems finish school with single-variable calculus already begun — treat "precalculus" and "first calculus exposure" as one continuous stage.

## Part 2: The university math-for-physics sequence

### 2a. The Khan Academy ladder (canonical free adult-relearning path)

Recommended order ([Khan Help Center](https://support.khanacademy.org/hc/en-us/community/posts/360013315632-Order-of-progression-through-math-classes)): Arithmetic → Pre-algebra → Algebra 1 → Geometry → Algebra 2 → Trigonometry → Precalculus → AP Calculus AB/BC → Multivariable calculus → Differential equations / Linear algebra / Statistics in any order. Khan's Multivariable course covers partials, gradient, divergence/curl, double/triple/line/surface integrals, and Green's/Stokes'/divergence theorems; its DE and Linear Algebra courses are lighter than a university course — finish with MIT OCW 18.03/18.06 (Strang) or the Boas/RHB chapters.

### 2b. University core sequence (MIT numbering)
1. Single-variable calculus (18.01) → 2. Multivariable calculus (18.02, through Stokes) → 3. Differential equations (18.03) → 4. Linear algebra (18.06) → 5. A "math methods for physics" consolidation (Boas-level) plus optional advanced topics (complex analysis, PDEs, group theory, differential geometry).

### 2c. The math-methods canon — verified tables of contents

**Boas, *Mathematical Methods in the Physical Sciences*, 3rd ed. (Wiley, 2006)** — the best single target for an adult ("know everything in Boas and you have the math for an undergrad physics degree"). 15 chapters: 1. Infinite Series, Power Series · 2. Complex Numbers · 3. Linear Algebra · 4. Partial Differentiation · 5. Multiple Integrals · 6. Vector Analysis · 7. Fourier Series and Transforms · 8. ODEs (incl. Laplace transforms, Dirac delta, Green functions) · 9. Calculus of Variations · 10. Tensor Analysis · 11. Special Functions · 12. Series Solutions; Legendre, Bessel, Hermite, Laguerre · 13. PDEs (Laplace, heat/Schrödinger, wave; separation of variables in three coordinate systems) · 14. Complex Variables (residues, conformal mapping) · 15. Probability and Statistics. ([TOC](https://sites.evergreen.edu/psamm2425/wp-content/uploads/sites/756/2024/09/Boas-Preface-ToC.pdf))

**Riley, Hobson & Bence, *Mathematical Methods for Physics and Engineering*, 3rd ed. (Cambridge, 2006)** — most comprehensive single volume; starts lower (school algebra) and ends higher (representation theory). 31 chapters: preliminary algebra; preliminary calculus; complex numbers; series; partial differentiation; multiple integrals; vector algebra; matrices and vector spaces; normal modes; vector calculus; line/surface/volume integrals; Fourier series; integral transforms; ODEs (first-order, higher-order, series solutions); eigenfunction methods; special functions; quantum operators; PDEs (two chapters); calculus of variations; integral equations; complex variables (two chapters); tensors; numerical methods; **group theory; representation theory**; probability; statistics. Its chapters 1–2 double as a diagnostic: if "Preliminary algebra/calculus" feels hard, drop back to the school stages. ([Publisher](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/mathematical-methods-for-physics-and-engineering/911A43AE1CF224743D32707FCC4AE0EB))

**Arfken, Weber & Harris, *Mathematical Methods for Physicists*, 7th ed. (Elsevier, 2013)** — the graduate-leaning reference; use for depth after Boas. 20 chapters incl. tensors and differential forms, Sturm–Liouville, Green's functions, gamma/Bessel/Legendre, angular momentum, group theory, Fourier/integral transforms.

The shared core of all three books: vector calculus, linear algebra with eigenproblems, ODEs, Fourier methods, PDEs by separation of variables, complex analysis with residues, calculus of variations, special functions, tensors, probability, group theory.

## Part 3: Physics ↔ math dependency table

Anchored on MIT's published prerequisites (8.02←Calc I; 8.03←Calc II; 8.04←8.03+18.03; 8.044 and 8.07←18.03) — see [MIT Course 8 catalog](https://catalog.mit.edu/subjects/8/).

| Physics stage | Math required BEFORE | Where it is load-bearing |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday quantitative reasoning (density, speed, unit conversions) | Fractions, decimals, percent, ratio/rate, proportional reasoning | d = m/V and v = d/t are ratio statements; unit conversion is multiplication by 1 |
| Conceptual/algebra-based physics | Algebra 1, linear & quadratic graphs, scientific notation, units | Kinematics equations are quadratics; slope = velocity, area = displacement prefigure calculus |
| Vectors, projectiles, statics, waves intro | Right-triangle trig, unit circle, sinusoid graphs, vector components | Every force decomposition is trig; y = A sin(kx − ωt) is a transformed sinusoid |
| Newtonian mechanics properly (8.01, Kleppner) | Single-variable calculus; Taylor-series intuition | F = ma is a differential equation; work = ∫F dx; sin θ ≈ θ |
| Electromagnetism I (8.02) | Calc fluency; multivariable concepts (flux) at least concurrently | Gauss's law is a surface integral; RC/LC circuits are first ODEs |
| Waves & vibrations (8.03) | ODEs (2nd-order driven/damped), complex exponentials, Fourier series, linear algebra for normal modes | e^{iωt} replaces trig bookkeeping; normal modes are an eigenvalue problem |
| Lagrangian/Hamiltonian mechanics | Multivariable calculus, calculus of variations, linear algebra (inertia tensor), ODEs | Principle of least action; rigid-body rotation needs eigenvectors |
| Full Maxwell E&M (Griffiths) | **Vector calculus mastery** (div/grad/curl, Stokes/divergence theorems, curvilinear coordinates); PDEs; Legendre polynomials; Fourier | Maxwell's equations ARE vector calculus; boundary-value problems = Laplace in spherical coordinates |
| Quantum mechanics I (8.04, Griffiths) | ODEs (hard prereq), linear algebra (operators, eigenvalues), complex numbers, Fourier transforms, PDE separation, probability | Schrödinger is a PDE; stationary states are eigenfunctions; momentum space = Fourier transform |
| QM II/III (Dirac formalism) | Serious linear algebra: Hilbert spaces, Hermitian/unitary operators, change of basis; some group theory (SU(2)) | State = vector, observable = Hermitian operator |
| Statistical mechanics | Probability & combinatorics (distributions, Stirling), multivariable calculus (exact differentials, Lagrange multipliers), Gaussian integrals | Entropy = log of multiplicity; Maxwell relations are partial-derivative identities |
| Optics/signal-level waves | Fourier analysis, convolution | Fraunhofer diffraction = Fourier transform of the aperture |
| Advanced E&M/QM (Green functions, scattering) | Complex analysis: residues, contour integration | Contour integrals evaluate propagators; Kramers–Kronig from analyticity |
| Special relativity | Linear algebra (Lorentz transformations as matrices), hyperbolic functions | Boosts are hyperbolic rotations; 4-vectors are matrix algebra |
| Particle physics / advanced symmetry | Group theory and representations (SU(2), SU(3)) | Symmetry classification; Noether's theorem |
| General relativity | Tensor analysis and differential geometry: metric, covariant derivatives, curvature; calculus of variations | Einstein's equations are tensor PDEs; geodesics extremize proper time |
| Experimental physics / labs | Statistics: propagation of uncertainty, least squares, chi-square | Every lab report |

## Part 4: What an adult can compress — and what must not be skipped

### Compress aggressively (weeks, not years)
- **All of primary arithmetic** — a numerate adult needs ~2–6 weeks of diagnostic-driven drill (use mastery quizzes to test out; skip the redundant instruction, never the diagnostic).
- Multi-digit hand algorithms (long division of 5-digit numbers): know the principle, use a calculator forever; estimation matters more.
- Synthetic-geometry **proof volume** and compass-straightedge constructions; competition geometry is entirely unnecessary for physics.
- Conic-section minutiae (keep basic ellipse/parabola/hyperbola forms for orbits).
- School statistics units (grades 6–9 data-handling) — redone properly at the stat-mech stage.
- Financial/consumer arithmetic; mensuration-formula memorization.
- Obscure trig identities — derive from Euler's formula when needed.
- Khan's DE and Linear Algebra courses are themselves compressed — treat them as on-ramps and finish with MIT OCW 18.03/18.06 or Boas/RHB chapters.

### Must NOT be skipped (the non-negotiables)
- **Proportional reasoning** — the best-documented adult gap; underlies density, Ohm's law, gas laws, inverse-square laws, dimensional analysis. If shaky here, everything after wobbles.
- **Fraction fluency** — physics adds 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ with symbols, where a calculator cannot rescue you.
- **Algebraic manipulation to automaticity** — every hesitation steals working memory from the physics. Includes exponent and log rules cold.
- **The unit circle, radian thinking, sinusoid graphs, core trig identities** — all of physics oscillates.
- **Function sense** — composition, inverses, transformations, qualitative graph reading.
- **Vectors** — components, dot and cross products; skipping this makes mechanics and E&M permanently harder.
- **Series and approximation** (binomial expansion, Taylor's idea) — "expand to first order" is the single most-used move in physics.
- **Working with units / dimensional analysis** — the physicist's first error-check.
- **Completing the square** — resurfaces in Gaussian integrals and the quantum harmonic oscillator.

### Realistic timeline for a committed adult
1. Weeks 0–6: diagnose and patch arithmetic → pre-algebra (heavy on fractions/ratio/percent)
2. Months 2–6: Algebra 1 + geometry essentials + Algebra 2 (interleaved)
3. Months 6–9: Trigonometry + Precalculus (vectors, series, limits)
4. Months 9–15: Calculus 1 and 2 (start calculus-based mechanics as soon as Calc 1 is done)
5. Months 15–24: Multivariable, ODEs, linear algebra interleaved with general physics (E&M after Calc 2 / during multivariable, per the MIT prerequisite structure)
6. Years 2–3: Boas cover-to-cover alongside intermediate physics; add RHB/Arfken chapters (group theory, tensors, complex analysis) as the physics demands them
